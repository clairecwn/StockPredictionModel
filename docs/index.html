How Settle works

When a company is in the news, its stock usually jumps or drops over the first day or two and then settles down. Settle finds the latest news for each stock and compares it with about 317,000 similar moments from 2001 to 2025. From those, it estimates what the next four weeks may bring.

The call

Buy means that in similar cases, buying right away worked out better than waiting. Wait means that waiting a few days usually got a better price. Use it to time a purchase you already plan, not as a reason to buy.

For stocks without recent news, the call comes from how the stock moved against the market over the last week.

How sure the call is

Slight lean: close to a coin flip. Mild lean: a small but steady edge. Clearer call: the most one-sided cases, about 1 in 5 calls.

The risks

The expected low is how far the price may still fall before it reaches its lowest point in the next four weeks. The bad case is a fall that only about 1 in 10 similar stocks went beyond.

How much to put in

Choose a budget and a risk level, and Settle suggests an amount small enough that a bad month stays within the loss you chose. Add up to three stocks to one plan. Their amounts shrink a little, because stocks often fall together.
