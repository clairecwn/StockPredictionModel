How Settle works

When a company is in the news, its stock usually jumps or drops over the first day or two and then settles down. Settle finds the latest news for each stock and compares it with about 317,000 similar moments from 2001 to 2025. From those, it estimates what the next four weeks may bring.

The call

Buy means that in similar cases, buying right away worked out better than waiting. Wait means that waiting a few days usually got a better price. The difference is small, so the call is best used to time a purchase you were already planning, not as a reason to buy. Stocks without recent news get no call, but their chart and risks are still shown.

The risks

The expected low is how far the price may still fall before it reaches its lowest point in the next four weeks. The bad case is a fall that only about 1 in 10 similar stocks went beyond. Both were tested on years the models never saw, and the bad case was passed about as often as promised.

How much to put in

Choose a budget and a risk level, and Settle suggests an amount small enough that a bad month stays within the loss you chose. You can add up to three stocks to a plan. Their amounts shrink a little when combined, because stocks often fall together in a sell-off.
